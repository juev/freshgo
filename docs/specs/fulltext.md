# Full text of articles

Status: implemented.
Sources: user requests of 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-08 (issue #29) and the decisions recorded in `plan.md`; `FreshRSS_Entry::loadCompleteContent`, `getContentByParsing`, `originalContent` and `FreshRSS_http_Util::httpGet` of FreshRSS at commit `219eaf58`.

## Purpose and scope

For a feed that carries summaries, freshgo takes the text of each article from its web page: the elements a CSS selector picks, or the article a readability extraction finds without a selector. `internal/fulltext` reads a page; `internal/refresh` decides for which entries and where the text goes. Covers plan requirement R12.

## Requirements

- T1. A feed with a non-blank `pathEntries` (a CSS selector, or several separated by commas) has the pages of its entries read. The page of an entry is the one at its link, requested with the feed's own request settings (credentials, proxy, cookies, headers, user agent, time limit) and under the limits and the address rules of every request of freshgo.
- T2. Pages are read for new entries and for entries the feed has changed, never for an entry that is stored and unchanged. An entry without a link is left as it is.
- T3. With `path_entries_conditions`, a list of queries in the search language, a page is read only for an entry that matches at least one of them. Blank conditions do not count. A condition that cannot be used (`search.md`, Q10) is reported in the log and matches nothing.
- T4. A page that sends on with `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` is followed, up to four times in a row; the target is resolved against the address of the page.
- T5. The text is the HTML of every element the selector matches, in document order, an element inside another matched one included a second time. It is cleaned by the sanitizer of feed content; relative addresses are resolved against the `<base href>` of the page, else against the address the page finally came from.
- T6. With `path_entries_filter`, a CSS selector, a picked element that matches it is left out, and so is every matching element inside the others, both before the cleaning and after it, so that the filter may name what the page has and what the cleaned markup has (`data-sanitized-class`).
- T7. The text is stored between the markers `<!-- FULLCONTENT start //-->` and `<!-- FULLCONTENT end //-->`. By `content_action` of the feed it replaces the text of the feed (`replace`, also when the attribute is absent), which then goes to `original_content` in the attributes of the entry, or stands before it (`prepend`) or after it (`append`).
- T8. Whatever goes wrong, an unusable selector, a failed request, an empty page, no element matched, leaves the text of the feed, is reported in the log and does not fail the refresh of the feed.
- T9. A feed without `pathEntries` and with `path_entries_filter` has the matching elements cut out of the text the feed gives. When something was cut, the text as it came goes to `original_content`.
- T10. The change hash of an entry covers the text the feed gave, not the text of the page: an entry is not taken as changed because its stored text is the page's.
- T11. A feed with `path_entries_auto` set to true in its attributes has the pages of its entries read without a selector: the article is the one a readability extraction finds on the page, as the reader view of a browser does. `pathEntries` is then not looked at, not even for whether it can be read. Everything else is as with a selector: T1–T4, the cleaning and the base address of T5, T7, T8, T10. The filter of T6 is applied to the page before the article is looked for, since the extraction drops classes and identifiers, and to the cleaned markup after it. A page on which no article is found is a failure of T8, and so is one whose article has less than 250 characters of text: a page that is an application has the few words of its loading screen and no article.
- T12. When the way a feed gets the text of its entries is changed in its form (`web.md`, U43: the selector, `path_entries_auto`, `path_entries_filter`, `path_entries_conditions`, `content_action`), the unread entries of the feed are brought in line with it at once (`Refresher.CompleteUnread`): each has the text the feed gave put back, from `original_content` or by cutting the marked text out, and is then completed as a new entry would be. So turning full text off gives them the text of the feed again. An entry whose page cannot be read keeps the text it had; a read entry is not touched; an entry whose text changed gets `last_modified` of now.
- T13. One entry takes the article of its page on request (`Refresher.CompleteEntry`, `web.md` U97), whatever its feed says about full text: the article is found without a selector (T11), without what `path_entries_filter` of the feed names, requested with the request settings of the feed. It replaces the text of the feed, which goes to `original_content`, whatever `content_action` says; an entry that has the text of its page already has the text of the feed put back first. The same request with the other value puts the text of the feed back. A page that gives no article, and an entry without a link, leave the entry as it is and are an error. The entry gets `last_modified` of now. A refresh of the unchanged feed leaves the text of the page (T2); a change of the entry in the feed replaces it as for any entry of the feed.
- T14. With a browser named to the server (`-browser-url`, the websocket of the DevTools protocol of a browser that runs already), a feed with `page_by_browser` set to true in its attributes has its pages read by that browser in place of a request (`internal/browser`), at a refresh (T1, T11), on request for one entry (T13) and in the preview of the form. The browser opens the page in a tab of its own, in a browser context of its own that is thrown away with the tab, so that no page finds a cookie of an earlier one, one page at a time, and gives the markup the page ends with and the address it ends at, which is the base of T5; what the page sent it on to, it has followed, so T4 does not apply. The address of the entry is held to the address rules of a request first (`fetch.Client.Allowed`); the request settings of the feed, the proxy of the installation and the limits of a request do not apply to the browser, which goes its own way to the network. A page has 30 seconds and may have 16 million characters of markup. A page that still shows the check of Cloudflare when its time is over (it defines `_cf_chl_opt`) is an error of its own, `browser.ErrChallenge`, and like every other failure leaves the text the entry had (T8, T13). Without a browser the attribute is kept and does nothing: the page is requested.

## Decisions

- **CSS selectors are applied by `goquery`** (`cascadia`), not translated to XPath as FreshRSS does with `phpgt/cssxpath`. The result is compared with FreshRSS for the kinds of selectors listed under verification.
- **Pages are read before anything else looks at the entry**: hooks, auto-read rules and filter actions see the completed text, for new and for changed entries alike. FreshRSS does so for new entries.
- **The automatic extraction is `codeberg.org/readeck/go-readability/v2`**, the maintained fork of `go-shiori/go-readability`, a port of Readability.js of Mozilla. It brings three modules into the build: `go-shiori/dom`, `itlightning/dateparse` and `gogs/chardet`. What it returns is cleaned by the sanitizer of feed content like any other text of a page.
- **The automatic way is an attribute of its own**, not a reserved value of the selector: a selector the user wrote stays in the form while the automatic way is on.
- **Only unread entries follow a change of the settings** (T12): what the reader has read stays as it was read. Fetching a feed anew still reads the pages of its ten newest entries, read or not, and only while pages are read.
- **The pages of T12 are read while the form is being saved**, one after the other, and the reader waits for the answer; entries are stored one by one, so a request that is given up keeps what was done.
- **An article has 250 characters of text at the least.** Of twelve real pages, the four without an article gave 42 to 156 characters (a game, a page drawn by a script, an item of a list of links, a placeholder), the shortest of eight articles 1284. `readability.CheckDocument`, the test of Mozilla for a page that can be read, does not tell them apart: it took the game for readable and an essay of 18 000 characters set with line breaks for not. A note shorter than that which a feed links to keeps the text of the feed.
- **Pages are not cached.** FreshRSS keeps a fetched page on disk for the cache duration; here a page is read at most once per new or changed entry.
- **A relative `<base href>` is resolved against the address of the page.**
- **The request settings of the feed go with the request for the page whatever host the page is on**, as in FreshRSS: credentials, cookies and headers, and the POST body of a feed that is fetched with POST. Cookies for a site whose feed lives on another host are what the setting is used for; the other side of it is that a feed decides, by its links, where the credentials set for it are sent.
- **A browser is reached through the DevTools protocol**, with `chromedp` and its module `remote`, not through the API of a solver such as FlareSolverr. Tried on 2026-10-09 on an article of `openai.com`, which answers a request with 403 and `cf-mitigated: challenge`: Lightpanda got it in 2 to 6 seconds with 32 MiB idle and 230 to 350 MiB at the peak, the headless shell of Chrome with `--disable-blink-features=AutomationControlled` with 70 MiB and about 300 MiB, FlareSolverr with 750 MiB and Byparr with 1400 MiB at the peak. Both browsers speak the protocol, so which one runs is a matter of the deployment.
- **Nothing of the feed goes to the browser**, neither its proxy nor its user agent: in the trial the check was not passed through the proxy of the installation, and a check looks at whether the browser is what it says it is.
- **Every page has a browser context of its own.** In the trial the headless shell of Chrome was let through to the first page and stayed behind the check on every later one, three times of three, until each page got a context of its own, with which it was let through three times of three. Lightpanda, which has a session for each connection, was let through either way.
- **Where the browser goes is checked once**, for the address of the entry. What the page then loads, and where it sends the browser on, is not checked through the protocol: the browser is to be kept away from internal networks by the deployment.
- **A page the browser got with an error status is not told apart** from one it got with 200: the status is not asked for. An error page gives no article without a selector (T11), and with one it gives what the selector matches there.
- **A name of the browser is turned into an address before it is asked for its socket**, each of its addresses in turn: Chrome answers `/json/version` only to a request that names it by address, and a name can have addresses the browser does not listen at. Of what the browser calls its socket only the path is taken, since it names the host it knows itself by.

## Known differences from FreshRSS

- When the filter removes something from the cleaned markup, FreshRSS 1.30.1 returns the content inside a `<body>` element; freshgo returns the content alone. Seen in the reference (`filter by attribute`).
- For a changed entry FreshRSS takes the text it has stored, the previous full text, as the text of the feed: with `replace` it lands in `original_content`, with `append` the new text is added to the old. freshgo uses what the feed gives now. Read from the code, not run.
- The four refreshes of T4 are not reduced by HTTP redirects on the way, which FreshRSS counts together. Read from the code, not run.

## Verification scenarios

- T5, T6, T4: `TestAgainstFreshRSS` in `internal/fulltext` reads the pages of `testdata/reference/oracle/articles/` with the 29 cases of `fulltext-cases.json` and expects what `getContentByParsing` of FreshRSS 1.30.1 returned (`fulltext.json`): descendant, child and sibling combinators, classes, identifiers, attribute selectors (`=`, `~=`, `|=`, `^=`, `$=`, `*=`, presence), `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `:nth-child`, `:nth-of-type`, `:not`, `*`, lists of selectors, nested matches, a filter, a `<base>` element, a meta refresh.
- R12, T1, T2, T7, T10: `TestFullText` in `internal/refresh`, for each `content_action` and with a filter: the stored content, `original_content`, no request and no change on a refresh of the unchanged feed, a new request when the entry changes.
- T3, T4, T8, T2: `TestFullTextConditionsAndFailures`: conditions, among them one that cannot be used, alone and next to a usable one; a page that is gone, one without the element, one that sends on, an entry without a link.
- T9: `TestContentFilterWithoutFullText`.
- T13: `TestCompleteEntry` in `internal/refresh` (the article in place of the summary with the filter of the feed applied, asked twice, a refresh of the unchanged feed, the text of the feed back, a page without an article, an entry without a link, an entry there is not, the entry of another user).
- T14: `TestReadInPlaceOfARequest` in `internal/fulltext` (no request, the base address, a page that sends on, an empty page, the error of the reader); `TestAllowed` in `internal/fetch`; `TestPagesByBrowser` in `internal/refresh` (the setting without a browser, a page read by the browser and not requested, an internal address not handed over, a feed without the setting, one entry on request, the preview, a failure of the browser); `TestNew` and `TestSocket` in `internal/browser`; with a real Chrome, `make test-e2e`, `TestE2EPage` (a script run, a page that sends on, a check that is passed, one that is not, a second visit that finds no cookie of the first, a page over the limit, one that cannot be reached, a request given up, no tab left open). - T12: `TestCompleteUnread` in `internal/refresh` (nothing for a feed that reads no pages, the unread entries completed and a read one left, a page that is gone, the text of the feed back when full text is turned off); the form in `TestFeedActions`.
- T11: `TestAutomatic` in `internal/fulltext` (the article without the menu, the column and the footer of its page, without scripts, addresses resolved, a filter, a meta refresh, a `<base>` element, a page without an article, a page with a few words and no article, a selector that is not CSS passed over); `TestFullTextAutomatic` in `internal/refresh` (the stored content between its markers, `original_content`, a page without an article and an entry without a link, the log, no request on a refresh of the unchanged feed); `TestPreviewArticle`. Not automated: the extraction was tried by hand on three articles of real sites (go.dev, simonwillison.net, theverge.com) and gave their text.
